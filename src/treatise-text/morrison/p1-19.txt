A type of curved lines, like the legs of a spider or a woman’s hairnet, come from the zenith and cross the almucantars at right angles. These curves or divisions are called azimuths and the divide the horizon of your astrolabe into 24 parts. The azimuths show directions and other results, such as finding the hour angle of the Sun and every star. (ed. Chaucer uses the term ‘cenyth’ which is the point on the horizon corresponding to an azimuth angle. This is not exactly hour angle, but it is probably what was meant.).
